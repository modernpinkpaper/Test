## Caption
He didn’t ask for a discount. He just said “mmm.” Chase Hughes tells a story about quoting a client $6,000 for his work. The client didn’t reject the price. He didn’t negotiate. He simply made a sound. And Hughes immediately started lowering his price. $6K → $5K → 50% off → even 75% off. This is what he calls collapse on the Composure Rating Scale. The scale moves from: COLLAPSE → COMPOSURE → POSTURING And Hughes argues that composure isn’t just about appearing confident. He breaks authority down into five qualities: • Confidence • Discipline • Leadership • Gratitude • Enjoyment Your weakest area can show up in the way you communicate, breathe, move, posture, and interact with other people. That’s why someone can read every article about “looking confident,” fix their posture, make better eye contact, shake hands correctly — and still give people the feeling that something is off. Because your internal state eventually bleeds into your external behavior. Hughes explains that we’re constantly manufacturing gut feelings in other people, whether we realize it or not. And those feelings can be influenced by five areas of our lives: Environment. Time. Appearance. Social life. Finances. The interesting part is that he doesn’t tell you to immediately “fix” everything. He tells you to measure it first. Notice where you fall on the scale. Notice when you collapse. Notice when you posture. Notice what changes your composure. Awareness comes before correction. This is one of the concepts Hughes breaks down in the Behavior Ops Manual. #socialskills #selfmastery #Composure #negotiation #HumanBehavior
