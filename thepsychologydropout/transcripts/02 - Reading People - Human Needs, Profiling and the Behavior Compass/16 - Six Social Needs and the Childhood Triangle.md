## Caption
What if the way someone needs to be seen today can tell you something about what was missing yesterday? In the Behavior Ops Manual, Chase Hughes breaks down six social needs: significance, approval, acceptance, intelligence, pity, and power. The key distinction? It’s not necessarily about what someone is—it’s about what they need other people to see them as. That can lead you backward toward the Childhood Triangle: the experiences that may have shaped which needs became especially important to that person. The “tough guy” who constantly needs to look powerful may not actually need power. He may need you to see him as powerful. And those are two very different things.
