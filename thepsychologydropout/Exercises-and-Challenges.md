# Try This: Exercises and Challenges

Every hands-on exercise, challenge, or "write this down" moment from the 86 videos. Each one has the **steps**, **why it works** (in Chase's words), and the **video** it comes from.

Tip: Chase says to start small and do one thing at a time. Pick one exercise, practice it until it feels automatic, then add another.

## Self-Mastery and Confidence

### 1. The "Move Slower" Week
**Steps:**
1. For one week, focus on just one thing: can you move slower than the other people in the room?
2. Picture yourself standing in a swimming pool. However fast your arms and legs would move underwater, that's your "speed limit" for the week.

**Why:** "There's one thing that fear does, it speeds our body up." Changing your body changes how your emotions feel. It is step one toward composure and comfort. [#31 Compare Yourself on Comfort, Not Looks or Status](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/31%20-%20Compare%20Yourself%20on%20Comfort%2C%20Not%20Looks%20or%20Status.md) [#36 The Real Problem Is Comfort](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/36%20-%20The%20Real%20Problem%20Is%20Comfort.md)

### 2. Measure Your Composure (Collapse / Posturing)
**Steps:**
1. Don't try to fix anything yet. Just notice.
2. When do you **collapse** (make yourself small, give in, lower your price)?
3. When do you **posture** (make yourself big, loud, defensive)?
4. Notice what moves you toward calm **composure**.

**Why:** "Awareness comes before correction." You might collapse in some areas of life and posture in others. [#35 He Just Said Mmm - Collapse vs Composure](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/35%20-%20He%20Just%20Said%20Mmm%20-%20Collapse%20vs%20Composure.md) [#31 Compare Yourself on Comfort, Not Looks or Status](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/31%20-%20Compare%20Yourself%20on%20Comfort%2C%20Not%20Looks%20or%20Status.md)

### 3. The Authority Inventory (Find Your Lowest Point)
**Steps:**
1. Rate yourself on the five traits of authority: **confidence, discipline, leadership, gratitude, enjoyment.**
2. Also check the five areas that "bleed out": **environment, time, appearance, social life, finances.** (Is your room a mess? Are you always late? Are your money worries on your mind?)
3. Find your **lowest** score. Work on that one first.

**Why:** "Wherever your lowest point is, that's what's keeping you from being successful in most conversations." It's "the highest leverage thing." [#32 The Authority Inventory - Find Your Lowest Point](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/32%20-%20The%20Authority%20Inventory%20-%20Find%20Your%20Lowest%20Point.md) [#33 Why You Get a Bad Gut Feeling About Someone](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/33%20-%20Why%20You%20Get%20a%20Bad%20Gut%20Feeling%20About%20Someone.md) [#35 He Just Said Mmm - Collapse vs Composure](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/35%20-%20He%20Just%20Said%20Mmm%20-%20Collapse%20vs%20Composure.md)

### 4. Zoom Your Gratitude In and Out
**Steps:**
1. Zoom in: what are you grateful for today (family, a doctor's visit, medicine)?
2. Zoom out: your state, your country, even the solar system.
3. When stressed, change the "camera angle" on the problem: move it sideways, and move it higher.

**Why:** "Most people... with stress in their life is they're just zoomed in." [#34 The 5 Traits of Authority](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/34%20-%20The%205%20Traits%20of%20Authority.md)

### 5. The Confidence Creed (Say It Out Loud)
**Steps:** Read these lines aloud:
- "I forgive myself fast and forever, with no exceptions."
- "I choose roles with intention."
- "I will never wait for social permission or evidence. That all comes from inside of me."
- "I regulate before I perform."
- "My confidence is a habit, not a tactic."
- "I give myself full authority."

**Why:** Confidence "can never be reactive. It has to be engineered." [#38 Confidence Is a Skill, Not a Trait](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/38%20-%20Confidence%20Is%20a%20Skill%2C%20Not%20a%20Trait.md)

### 6. Step Into a Role
**Steps:** Before a hard moment, don't wait to *feel* confident. Decide on a role ("I am the leader in this meeting," "I am the expert here") and act from it.

**Why:** "If you see a firefighter run into a burning building, they're not doing it as Joe from the gym... The role is what gives you permission. The permission gives you composure, and the composure gives you power." [#38 Confidence Is a Skill, Not a Trait](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/38%20-%20Confidence%20Is%20a%20Skill%2C%20Not%20a%20Trait.md)

### 7. Practice Silence
**Steps:** This week, each time someone provokes you:
- don't respond,
- don't explain yourself,
- don't let them into your "emotional living room."

**Why:** Each time, "you're building muscle... authority muscle, self respect muscle." "Silence is your sword and self control is your shield." [#41 Silence Is Your Sword](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/41%20-%20Silence%20Is%20Your%20Sword.md)

### 8. State a Boundary the Right Way
**Steps:**
1. Say the boundary once, clearly.
2. Pause.
3. Move on. No story, no defense, no raised voice, no long explanation.

**Why:** "Explaining signals uncertainty... justifying signals that we can negotiate." "Reasonable people adjust immediately. Unreasonable people will escalate." [#40 Why Standing Up for Yourself Backfires](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/40%20-%20Why%20Standing%20Up%20for%20Yourself%20Backfires.md)

### 9. Find Where You Want to Be "Seen as Special"
**Steps:** Notice the ways you want other people to see you as special. Write them down as they pop up. The goal is to be aware of them and to stop needing them from others.

**Why:** "The absolute fastest way to become confident is to shift that mindset... into how unspecial I am." "I am not here to be understood. I am here to be true." [#39 Confidence Is a World View](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/39%20-%20Confidence%20Is%20a%20World%20View.md)

## Shame, Guilt and Forgiveness

### 10. "Pain as a Disguise for Virtue"
**Steps:** Write this sentence down: **"I am using pain as a disguise for virtue."**

**Why:** Your brain tricks you into believing "I feel terrible, therefore I must care." Chase says that's false. Guilt keeps you "the centre of your own pity party." [#51 Your Brain's Guilt Trick](transcripts/06%20-%20Shame%2C%20Guilt%20and%20Self-Forgiveness/51%20-%20Your%20Brain%27s%20Guilt%20Trick.md) [#49 Shame Is Not Morality (Full Talk)](transcripts/06%20-%20Shame%2C%20Guilt%20and%20Self-Forgiveness/49%20-%20Shame%20Is%20Not%20Morality%20%28Full%20Talk%29.md)

### 11. The Best Friend Test
**Steps:**
1. Picture your best friend coming to you today and confessing the exact thing you're ashamed of.
2. Would you forgive them?
3. Then ask: "So why don't you forgive you?"

**Why:** [#51 Your Brain's Guilt Trick](transcripts/06%20-%20Shame%2C%20Guilt%20and%20Self-Forgiveness/51%20-%20Your%20Brain%27s%20Guilt%20Trick.md) [#49 Shame Is Not Morality (Full Talk)](transcripts/06%20-%20Shame%2C%20Guilt%20and%20Self-Forgiveness/49%20-%20Shame%20Is%20Not%20Morality%20%28Full%20Talk%29.md)

### 12. The "I Forgive You" Body Check
**Steps:**
1. Think of the one thing you feel most shame or guilt about.
2. Feel it in your chest for a second. Notice your body: tighter jaw, shallow breathing, ribs coming down to protect your stomach.
3. Now say "I forgive you" (out loud or in your head) and really *feel* it, not "like a magical Harry Potter spell."
4. Notice: shoulders fall, face relaxes, jaw loosens, breathing gets deeper.

**Why:** "That's what authority feels like when it's not leaking." "You do not need to earn forgiveness." [#49 Shame Is Not Morality (Full Talk)](transcripts/06%20-%20Shame%2C%20Guilt%20and%20Self-Forgiveness/49%20-%20Shame%20Is%20Not%20Morality%20%28Full%20Talk%29.md)

## Discipline, Dopamine and Habits

### 13. The Dopamine Map
**Steps:**
1. On a blank journal page, list every place you get dopamine (pleasure) from. Be brutally honest: social media comments, alcohol, porn, video games, kids, work, dogs, creative work.
2. You have **100 points**. Spend all of them across your list, based on how much pleasure each gives you now.
3. On a new page, draw your **desired** map: where you *want* your dopamine to come from.
4. Draw arrows from the negative sources to the positive ones. "Borrow" points. You don't have to quit fully. Example: if one is 12 and one is 50, aim for the middle.

**Why:** "Dopamine is not happiness. Dopamine is pleasure." "Once people see that metric, it is very, very hard to ignore." "Your number one source of dopamine should be you." [#43 Map Your Dopamine and Shift the Points](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/43%20-%20Map%20Your%20Dopamine%20and%20Shift%20the%20Points.md) [#46 The Dopamine Map](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/46%20-%20The%20Dopamine%20Map.md)

### 14. Become Your Own Butler
**Steps:** Each evening, set things up for "future you": clothes laid out, breakfast ingredients ready, checklist for the day written, bag by the door, car clean. Start small.

**Why:** Discipline is "your ability to prioritise the needs of your future self." You want to look back "with gratitude" instead of regret, so "past tense me is becoming a source of dopamine for present tense me." [#44 Discipline Means Putting Your Future Self First](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/44%20-%20Discipline%20Means%20Putting%20Your%20Future%20Self%20First.md) [#45 Become Your Own Butler](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/45%20-%20Become%20Your%20Own%20Butler.md)

### 15. Identity Disgust Statements
**Steps:**
1. Stop saying "I'm working on myself" or "I'm trying to change."
2. Instead, when the old habit shows up, say: "That version is beneath me." "That behavior is unbelievably embarrassing." "I don't recognize myself when I act like that."

**Why:** "You have an identity that produces predictable behavior." Embarrassment, disgust and aversion are the three things that change identity fast. [#47 Habits Come From Identity](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/47%20-%20Habits%20Come%20From%20Identity.md)

### 16. The "This Is Not Me" Test
**Steps:** Picture waking up as an Olympic athlete who is suddenly 295 pounds. How fast would you get back in shape? Find a habit where you can honestly feel "this is not me."

**Why:** "Anytime you're feeling, this is not me... it's the most powerful motivator." [#48 Identity Is the Fastest Behavior Hack](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/48%20-%20Identity%20Is%20the%20Fastest%20Behavior%20Hack.md)

## Reading People

### 17. Learn One Body-Language Cue at a Time
**Steps:** Pick one cue (start with **blink rate**). Watch for it in every conversation until you notice it "without really trying." Only then add a second one (like **lip compression**).

**Why:** "It's just kind of like learning to drive a car... you wanna minimise all of this stuff you're trying to pay attention to at one time." [#24 Blink Rate and Lip Compression](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/24%20-%20Blink%20Rate%20and%20Lip%20Compression.md) [#25 Blink Rate - An Easy Shift to Miss](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/25%20-%20Blink%20Rate%20-%20An%20Easy%20Shift%20to%20Miss.md)

### 18. Rewind After Lip Compression
**Steps:** When you see someone press their lips together, mentally "rewind": what were they just talking about right before?

**Why:** Lip compression usually means a withheld opinion or feeling. The topic right before it is where to pay attention. [#27 Lip Compression in Venture Capital Pitches](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/27%20-%20Lip%20Compression%20in%20Venture%20Capital%20Pitches.md) [#30 Lip Compression vs Lip Retraction](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/30%20-%20Lip%20Compression%20vs%20Lip%20Retraction.md)

### 19. Make It a Movie, Not a Photo
**Steps:** When you judge someone's body language, don't freeze one moment in your mind. Replay it like a short video and look for **change**.

**Why:** "You wanna watch for change." One still frame tells you almost nothing. [#29 How to Spot a Narcissist on the First Date](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/29%20-%20How%20to%20Spot%20a%20Narcissist%20on%20the%20First%20Date.md) [#23 Detecting Change Beats Memorizing Body Language](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/23%20-%20Detecting%20Change%20Beats%20Memorizing%20Body%20Language.md)

### 20. Profile Someone With the Behavior Compass
**Steps:** In your next conversation, try to spot:
1. Their **need** (significance, acceptance, approval, intelligence, pity, strength)
2. Their **decision pillar** (deviance, novelty, conformity, necessity, social, investment)
3. Their **value** (freedom, recognition, connection, growth, experiences, information)

**Why:** Chase says you can do it in under 60 seconds and know the person's mind "better than their own family." [#21 The Behavior Compass in Under 60 Seconds](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/21%20-%20The%20Behavior%20Compass%20in%20Under%2060%20Seconds.md) [#13 The Hidden Layer - Needs, Decision Pillars, Values](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/13%20-%20The%20Hidden%20Layer%20-%20Needs%2C%20Decision%20Pillars%2C%20Values.md) [#20 The Needs, Decision and Values Maps](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/20%20-%20The%20Needs%2C%20Decision%20and%20Values%20Maps.md)

### 21. Ask "What Do They Need Me to See?"
**Steps:** When someone shows off (the truck covered in tough-guy stickers, the loud person), ask: "What does this person need me to see them as?" Then ask: "What might have been missing in their childhood?"

**Why:** "He doesn't have a need to be powerful... He has a need for you to see him as powerful." [#14 What Someone Needs You to SEE](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/14%20-%20What%20Someone%20Needs%20You%20to%20SEE.md) [#16 Six Social Needs and the Childhood Triangle](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/16%20-%20Six%20Social%20Needs%20and%20the%20Childhood%20Triangle.md)

## Influence and Communication

### 22. The Alignment Week
**Steps:** For one week:
- Don't convince, push, or sell anybody anything.
- Try not to posture.
- In conversations: slow your breath, match their rhythm a little, "get curious instead of clever."
- **"Listen so hard that you forget what you're gonna say."**
- Try to find their need, decision pillar and value, and help those line up.

**Why:** Compliance only "pauses resistance." Alignment "dissolves" it. [#54 Alignment vs Compliance (Full Lesson)](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/54%20-%20Alignment%20vs%20Compliance%20%28Full%20Lesson%29.md) [#60 Alignment vs Compliance (Short)](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/60%20-%20Alignment%20vs%20Compliance%20%28Short%29.md)

### 23. The Energy Test
**Steps:** When someone agrees with you, notice: does the energy in the room go **up** (they lean in) or **down**?

**Why:** Up = real alignment. Down = they're just complying, and they'll "vanish the second you leave the room." [#54 Alignment vs Compliance (Full Lesson)](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/54%20-%20Alignment%20vs%20Compliance%20%28Full%20Lesson%29.md)

### 24. Respond With Statements, Not Questions (Elicitation)
**Steps:** Instead of asking a direct question, try:
- a wrong guess they'll want to correct ("I heard everyone here got bumped to $25 an hour"),
- a statement like "I bet that's challenging" or "I can imagine you enjoy that,"
- a little disbelief: "No way. There's no way that happened that fast."

**Why:** People have a natural need "to correct the record." "The more sensitive the information that you need, the less questions you should be asking." [#52 Elicitation - Getting Info Without Asking Questions](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/52%20-%20Elicitation%20-%20Getting%20Info%20Without%20Asking%20Questions.md)

### 25. Write Down "I Will Be Transferring Permission"
**Steps:** Write this in your notes: **"I will be transferring permission to others."**

**Why:** In the PCP model (perception, context, permission), permission is "the most important thing." [#53 The PCP Model of Influence](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/53%20-%20The%20PCP%20Model%20of%20Influence.md)

### 26. The Frame Check
**Steps:** Before a big conversation, rate yourself:
- **Belief:** How strongly do I believe in my view of what's going on?
- **Composure:** How calm am I?
- **Neediness:** How much do I need from this?

Frame = Belief × Composure ÷ Neediness. Raise the first two, lower the last.

**Why:** "They need nothing from the room. That is true power." [#58 Four NCI Methods - Frame, Status, Micro-Behavior, Compliance Ladder](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/58%20-%20Four%20NCI%20Methods%20-%20Frame%2C%20Status%2C%20Micro-Behavior%2C%20Compliance%20Ladder.md) [#59 Observation, Communication, Self-Mastery and Your Frame](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/59%20-%20Observation%2C%20Communication%2C%20Self-Mastery%20and%20Your%20Frame.md)

### 27. Try the "Stories" Phrase
**Steps:** In a real conversation, say: **"Most people are just living out stories that they didn't even write."** Watch the reaction.

**Why:** Chase says it "bypasses the brain's defenses and goes straight to identity." [#58 Four NCI Methods - Frame, Status, Micro-Behavior, Compliance Ladder](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/58%20-%20Four%20NCI%20Methods%20-%20Frame%2C%20Status%2C%20Micro-Behavior%2C%20Compliance%20Ladder.md)

## Media and Manipulation

### 28. "How Is This Trying to Make Me Feel?"
**Steps:** Ask this one question about everything: news, ads, social media posts, politicians, movies, even bumper stickers. Practice until it's automatic. You can teach it to kids.

**Why:** Chase says it's "100% effective" for "escaping the matrix." It helps you "float above instead of getting dragged down into... the feeling." [#75 How Is This Trying to Make Me Feel](transcripts/11%20-%20Media%2C%20Propaganda%20and%20Psyops/75%20-%20How%20Is%20This%20Trying%20to%20Make%20Me%20Feel.md)

### 29. Listen for Copy-Paste Phrases
**Steps:** When you watch news, notice when many outlets use the *exact* same phrase (like "unprecedented threat" or "undeniable evidence").

**Why:** "The moment you hear copy paste phrases, that is a [psyop]." [#74 How to Tell If You Are in a Psyop](transcripts/11%20-%20Media%2C%20Propaganda%20and%20Psyops/74%20-%20How%20to%20Tell%20If%20You%20Are%20in%20a%20Psyop.md)

### 30. See the Human, Not the Cartoon
**Steps:** Next time you argue with someone on the other political side (like that uncle at Thanksgiving), remind yourself you're "both being played."

**Why:** "The second that you look across the aisle and see a fellow human being instead of some caricature cartoon, is the moment that the scam starts to collapse." [#78 You and Your Uncle Are Not Enemies](transcripts/11%20-%20Media%2C%20Propaganda%20and%20Psyops/78%20-%20You%20and%20Your%20Uncle%20Are%20Not%20Enemies.md)

## Awareness and Perception

### 31. Refuse to Name It
**Steps:**
1. Look at the closest object near you (a water bottle, a chair, your hand).
2. For a few seconds, **refuse** to name it. Don't say the word in your head. Don't categorize it.
3. Notice how fast your mind tries to name it, and then the sharp, present feeling when you don't.

**Why:** "Language is jumping in front of every experience." Without the label, "there's a sudden sharpness, increase of reality and clarity." [#72 Language Is a Parasite - Tongue](transcripts/10%20-%20Language%20and%20Perception%20-%20Tongue/72%20-%20Language%20Is%20a%20Parasite%20-%20Tongue.md) [#73 The Hidden Power of Language - Tongue](transcripts/10%20-%20Language%20and%20Perception%20-%20Tongue/73%20-%20The%20Hidden%20Power%20of%20Language%20-%20Tongue.md)

### 32. Remember a Wordless Moment
**Steps:** Think of the most real moment of your life (a birth, a death, a breakup, deep awe). Notice that there were no words in that moment. Words only came after.

**Why:** "Every moment that you felt truly alive in your life was so much bigger than the language that you used to describe it." [#72 Language Is a Parasite - Tongue](transcripts/10%20-%20Language%20and%20Perception%20-%20Tongue/72%20-%20Language%20Is%20a%20Parasite%20-%20Tongue.md)

### 33. The Childhood Contract Reflection
**Steps:** Close your eyes. Remember being 5 or 6, when you asked for something real (attention, comfort) and were told "stop." Notice the "contract" you may have signed: "I'll need less, I'll stay small." Then remind yourself: "You're not five. And you don't have to disappear to be loved."

**Why:** Chase uses this to show how influence reaches the emotional (limbic) brain, not the logical one. [#55 Influence Is a Weapon](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/55%20-%20Influence%20Is%20a%20Weapon.md)

### 34. Spot the Old Argument
**Steps:** After your next fight with a partner, ask: "Was this really about the dishes? Or was I re-living something from 10, 15, 35 years ago?"

**Why:** "You're arguing with a ghost that wasn't in the room." [#64 Your Last Argument Was Not About the Dishes](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/64%20-%20Your%20Last%20Argument%20Was%20Not%20About%20the%20Dishes.md)

### 35. Check Your Childhood Triangle
**Steps:** When you overreact, ask which side got hit:
- **Reward** (boss ignored your idea — like not getting picked for kickball)
- **Safety** (partner criticized you — like waiting to get grounded)
- **Friend** (you feel unworthy at a party)

**Why:** "You're running old software." [#65 The Childhood Development Triangle](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/65%20-%20The%20Childhood%20Development%20Triangle.md)
