# Stories and Real-World Examples

The stories, examples and case studies used in the videos. Stories make the ideas easier to remember and to explain to others. Each has **the story**, **the lesson**, and the **video**.

## Chase's Personal Stories

### The "Mmm" Phone Call
**Story:** Right after leaving the military, someone called to book Chase for a keynote speech. Chase said his rate was $6K, including travel. The caller didn't argue. He just said "mmm." Chase panicked and offered $5K, then 50% off, then 75% off — he almost offered to pay *them* to let him speak.
**Lesson:** That's **collapse**: making yourself small so others are comfortable. The goal is composure. [#31 Compare Yourself on Comfort, Not Looks or Status](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/31%20-%20Compare%20Yourself%20on%20Comfort%2C%20Not%20Looks%20or%20Status.md) [#35 He Just Said Mmm - Collapse vs Composure](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/35%20-%20He%20Just%20Said%20Mmm%20-%20Collapse%20vs%20Composure.md)

### The Moray Eel
**Story:** At 21, stationed at Pearl Harbor, Chase was snorkeling at Hanauma Bay ("Namame Bay" in the captions) and a moray eel bit deep into his arm. A month later he complained to his mentor about a mean coworker. The mentor asked, "Did he bite you in the arm?" Chase said the eel was "just a creature doing what it does based off of who it became." His mentor stared at him for 15 seconds until Chase realized that was true of the coworker too.
**Lesson:** Difficult people are reacting from how they grew up. Don't take it personally. [#63 The Moray Eel Story](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/63%20-%20The%20Moray%20Eel%20Story.md)

### Rejected at 19
**Story:** At 19, at Pearl Harbor, a girl turned Chase down. He went home and searched "how to tell when girls like you" so he wouldn't be rejected again. That led to body language, then human behavior.
**Lesson:** Seeing people's fears and insecurities "made them human to me" and helped his own social anxiety: "they're messed up too." [#3 How Rejection Led Chase to Body Language](transcripts/01%20-%20About%20Chase%20Hughes%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Ops%20Manual/03%20-%20How%20Rejection%20Led%20Chase%20to%20Body%20Language.md)

### The Brain Diagnosis
**Story:** Chase got a diagnosis of a brain disease (neurodegeneration). He feared he wouldn't live long or stay "lucid or sane," and wondered how his family would eat. So he wrote down every technique he'd ever learned, which became the *Behavior Ops Manual*.
**Lesson:** Why the book exists, and why he started sharing advanced tools publicly. [#2 Who Is Chase Hughes - The Behavior Ops Manual](transcripts/01%20-%20About%20Chase%20Hughes%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Ops%20Manual/02%20-%20Who%20Is%20Chase%20Hughes%20-%20The%20Behavior%20Ops%20Manual.md) [#6 The Personal Reason the BOM Exists](transcripts/01%20-%20About%20Chase%20Hughes%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Ops%20Manual/06%20-%20The%20Personal%20Reason%20the%20BOM%20Exists.md) [#8 Ending Human Powerlessness](transcripts/01%20-%20About%20Chase%20Hughes%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Ops%20Manual/08%20-%20Ending%20Human%20Powerlessness.md)

### Teaching His Daughter With Bumper Stickers
**Story:** When his daughter Charlotte was 6–8, they stopped behind a Subaru covered in stickers (marathon, surf shop, fraternity). He asked, "What do they want you to feel?" She said: "That they're a good person... I'm fun... I'm cool... I would make a good friend."
**Lesson:** The one question that protects you from manipulation: "How is this trying to make me feel?" [#75 How Is This Trying to Make Me Feel](transcripts/11%20-%20Media%2C%20Propaganda%20and%20Psyops/75%20-%20How%20Is%20This%20Trying%20to%20Make%20Me%20Feel.md)

### The Smoker in the Airport
**Story:** Chase says he still gets emails from strangers he met randomly, like in an airport, whom he helped quit smoking in a minute or two.
**Lesson:** Real influence feels "like a drug" and can change a life fast — which is why ethics matter. [#54 Alignment vs Compliance (Full Lesson)](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/54%20-%20Alignment%20vs%20Compliance%20%28Full%20Lesson%29.md) [#60 Alignment vs Compliance (Short)](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/60%20-%20Alignment%20vs%20Compliance%20%28Short%29.md)

## Client and Work Stories

### The Struggling CEO ($600K → $4M a Month)
**Story:** A business owner's employees openly mocked him in board meetings and his company was "going downhill so fast." Chase rated him on confidence, discipline, leadership, gratitude and enjoyment, found the lowest one, and worked on that first. Within a few months, monthly revenue went from about $600–800K to about $4 million.
**Lesson:** Fix your lowest authority trait first — that's the highest leverage. [#32 The Authority Inventory - Find Your Lowest Point](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/32%20-%20The%20Authority%20Inventory%20-%20Find%20Your%20Lowest%20Point.md)

### The Green Bean Lawsuit (Jury Selection)
**Story:** A grocery company was being sued because a woman slipped on a green bean. Chase (who says he knows "nothing about the law... but I know people," and offers a "200% money back guarantee") wanted jurors who believe they control their own lives. He asked each one: "How does a person catch a cold?" One blamed dirty kids on the escalator. Another said, "I didn't wash my hands... didn't take vitamins."
**Lesson:** A harmless question can reveal someone's whole worldview (locus of control) without tipping off the other side. [#84 David vs Goliath - Archetypes in Trial Psychology](transcripts/13%20-%20True%20Crime%20and%20Trial%20Psychology/84%20-%20David%20vs%20Goliath%20-%20Archetypes%20in%20Trial%20Psychology.md)

### David and Goliath in the Courtroom
**Story:** If he were on the injured woman's side, Chase would never say "David and Goliath." He'd sprinkle words like "giant," "someone small," "slingshot," and talk about the DMV and doctors who keep you waiting 45 minutes. Jurors would feel "big company crushing a little person" and finish the story themselves.
**Lesson:** Plant the middle of a familiar story, and people's brains supply the ending — "justice." [#84 David vs Goliath - Archetypes in Trial Psychology](transcripts/13%20-%20True%20Crime%20and%20Trial%20Psychology/84%20-%20David%20vs%20Goliath%20-%20Archetypes%20in%20Trial%20Psychology.md)

### The Venture Capital Pitch
**Story:** Chase trains investors who hear pitches. When a founder says, "All the financials are great... we've projected out a good next couple of quarters" while pressing their lips together, that's a signal.
**Lesson:** Lip compression = something withheld. Rewind to what was said right before. [#27 Lip Compression in Venture Capital Pitches](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/27%20-%20Lip%20Compression%20in%20Venture%20Capital%20Pitches.md)

### The Car Dealership Owner and the Intelligence Officers
**Story:** Whether the client is an intelligence officer or a guy who owns a car dealership, the first thing Chase gives them on day one is the same authority assessment.
**Lesson:** The same human factors decide outcomes everywhere. [#32 The Authority Inventory - Find Your Lowest Point](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/32%20-%20The%20Authority%20Inventory%20-%20Find%20Your%20Lowest%20Point.md) [#33 Why You Get a Bad Gut Feeling About Someone](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/33%20-%20Why%20You%20Get%20a%20Bad%20Gut%20Feeling%20About%20Someone.md) [#35 He Just Said Mmm - Collapse vs Composure](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/35%20-%20He%20Just%20Said%20Mmm%20-%20Collapse%20vs%20Composure.md) [#36 The Real Problem Is Comfort](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/36%20-%20The%20Real%20Problem%20Is%20Comfort.md)

## Examples He Uses to Teach

### Whole Foods: How Much Do You Make?
**Story:** Challenge: in 60 seconds, find out how much a produce worker earns without asking a single question. Ask her where the cucumbers are. While she walks you there, say, "I read everybody here got bumped up to $25 an hour. Congratulations!" She says, "What? No, we only make $19.50. Only managers make 25."
**Lesson:** People have a deep need to correct wrong information. That's elicitation. [#52 Elicitation - Getting Info Without Asking Questions](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/52%20-%20Elicitation%20-%20Getting%20Info%20Without%20Asking%20Questions.md)

### The Tough-Guy Truck
**Story:** A giant jacked-up pickup with "truck nuts" on the hitch, an "I fight MMA" sticker, and a Glock sticker.
**Lesson:** "He doesn't have a need to be powerful. He has a need for you to see him as powerful." That points to what was missing in childhood. [#14 What Someone Needs You to SEE](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/14%20-%20What%20Someone%20Needs%20You%20to%20SEE.md) [#16 Six Social Needs and the Childhood Triangle](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/16%20-%20Six%20Social%20Needs%20and%20the%20Childhood%20Triangle.md)

### The Chihuahua and the Porcupine
**Story:** A Chihuahua barks and snaps; a porcupine raises its spikes when you get close. Both act out of fear. A "Chihuahua human" may have been bullied or had a harsh parent and learned "if I get loud enough, I'll be safe." A "porcupine human" learned to hurt anyone who gets close.
**Lesson:** Aggression is often a fear behavior from a childhood "script." [#61 Empathy and the Animal Behavior Chart](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/61%20-%20Empathy%20and%20the%20Animal%20Behavior%20Chart.md) [#62 What Is a Bully (Part 1)](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/62%20-%20What%20Is%20a%20Bully%20%28Part%201%29.md)

### The Raging Driver on the Freeway
**Story:** The person cutting everyone off and acting crazy on the highway.
**Lesson:** "Somewhere in that person's life, there's a feeling of a lack of control." [#62 What Is a Bully (Part 1)](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/62%20-%20What%20Is%20a%20Bully%20%28Part%201%29.md)

### The Missing Woman on the News
**Story:** "Local woman reported missing... Witnesses say they saw her arguing with her boyfriend. Details at 11."
**Lesson:** Your brain decides the boyfriend did it — and thinks it was your own idea. Put two facts close together and people connect them. [#82 Let Them Connect the Dots - Lindsay Clancy Trial (Part A)](transcripts/13%20-%20True%20Crime%20and%20Trial%20Psychology/82%20-%20Let%20Them%20Connect%20the%20Dots%20-%20Lindsay%20Clancy%20Trial%20%28Part%20A%29.md) [#83 Making the Jury Connect the Dots Themselves (Part B)](transcripts/13%20-%20True%20Crime%20and%20Trial%20Psychology/83%20-%20Making%20the%20Jury%20Connect%20the%20Dots%20Themselves%20%28Part%20B%29.md)

### Interest Rates
**Story:** The moment you mention interest rates, the person's arms start moving in close to their body.
**Lesson:** Watch when the body starts or stops "protecting arteries." [#23 Detecting Change Beats Memorizing Body Language](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/23%20-%20Detecting%20Change%20Beats%20Memorizing%20Body%20Language.md)

### "How Do You Like Your New Job?"
**Story:** "Oh, it's great," with lips pressed together. Or in sales: "Would you say your credit score's pretty good?" "Oh, yeah, credit's great" + lip compression.
**Lesson:** Something's being held back. You may need to ask more questions later. [#24 Blink Rate and Lip Compression](transcripts/03%20-%20Body%20Language%20and%20Spotting%20Stress/24%20-%20Blink%20Rate%20and%20Lip%20Compression.md)

### The Gun and the Knife (Hypnosis)
**Story:** You'd never shoot someone. But if you truly believed they were coming at you with a knife — or that the gun was just a water gun — the context changes, and so does your "permission."
**Lesson:** Perception → context → permission (the PCP model). [#56 Can Hypnosis Make You Do Anything](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/56%20-%20Can%20Hypnosis%20Make%20You%20Do%20Anything.md)

### The Firefighter
**Story:** A firefighter running into a burning building isn't doing it as "Joe from the gym." They do it as "I am a firefighter."
**Lesson:** Roles give permission; permission gives composure. [#38 Confidence Is a Skill, Not a Trait](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/38%20-%20Confidence%20Is%20a%20Skill%2C%20Not%20a%20Trait.md)

### The Olympic Athlete at 295 Pounds
**Story:** Imagine you're an Olympic athlete and you wake up one morning weighing 295 pounds. You'd set records losing the weight.
**Lesson:** Not to "be healthy" — because "this is not me." Identity is the strongest motivator. [#48 Identity Is the Fastest Behavior Hack](transcripts/05%20-%20Discipline%2C%20Dopamine%2C%20Habits%20and%20Identity/48%20-%20Identity%20Is%20the%20Fastest%20Behavior%20Hack.md)

### Laundry Pile vs. Good Posture
**Story:** You read "19 ways to look more confident" on LinkedIn, fix your posture and eye contact — but at home there's an eight-foot pile of laundry, a nasty bed and dishes piled up.
**Lesson:** Part of your brain knows you're faking it, and people sense it. [#33 Why You Get a Bad Gut Feeling About Someone](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/33%20-%20Why%20You%20Get%20a%20Bad%20Gut%20Feeling%20About%20Someone.md) [#35 He Just Said Mmm - Collapse vs Composure](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/35%20-%20He%20Just%20Said%20Mmm%20-%20Collapse%20vs%20Composure.md)

### Kickball, Grounding and the Party
**Story:** Your boss ignores your idea and it feels like not getting picked for kickball. Your partner criticizes you and you're seven, waiting to get grounded. You walk into a party and instantly feel unworthy.
**Lesson:** The Childhood Development Triangle: reward, safety, friend. [#65 The Childhood Development Triangle](transcripts/08%20-%20Childhood%2C%20Fear%2C%20Bullies%20and%20Empathy/65%20-%20The%20Childhood%20Development%20Triangle.md)

### The Sales Call That Sounds Like a Sales Call
**Story:** "Oh, hi, Mr. Hughes, this is [name] from [company]..." — your brain checks out instantly.
**Lesson:** If you sound like everything else, people tune out. Speak to their needs instead. [#11 Why We Hang Up on Sales Calls](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/11%20-%20Why%20We%20Hang%20Up%20on%20Sales%20Calls.md)

### The Sales Letter and the Networking Event
**Story:** For a sales letter, Chase ranks the six axes: suggestibility first, then focus, connection, expectancy, compliance, and openness last ("I'm selling to your mask"). At a networking event, the awkward person is already focused (worried about posture and teeth); connection is the hard part.
**Lesson:** Choose which axis to raise based on the situation. [#57 The FATE Model and the Six-Axis Model](transcripts/07%20-%20Influence%2C%20Persuasion%20and%20Hypnosis/57%20-%20The%20FATE%20Model%20and%20the%20Six-Axis%20Model.md)

## Interviews and Live Demos

### Profiling Steven Bartlett
**Story:** Asked "Who are you and what's your mission?", Bartlett listed podcaster, entrepreneur, investor — and boyfriend — and said his mission was "to pursue my potential and follow my curiosity." Chase noticed he didn't say "TV star," and that the mission was about himself, not others.
**Lesson:** His words point to a **significance** need, so "you make a difference" lands harder than "everyone likes you." [#12 Breaking Down Steven Bartlett's Hidden Needs](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/12%20-%20Breaking%20Down%20Steven%20Bartlett%27s%20Hidden%20Needs.md)

### Predicting Jack Kneel's Insecurities
**Story:** After about two hours together, Chase wrote three of host Jack Kneel's biggest insecurities on a notepad: worrying about how he's perceived, replaying what he could have said differently, and using constant sound/video when alone to avoid deep thoughts. Jack said he "nailed" them.
**Lesson:** Rapid profiling (Six-Minute X-Ray) in action. [#18 Profiling Jack Kneel in Minutes](transcripts/02%20-%20Reading%20People%20-%20Human%20Needs%2C%20Profiling%20and%20the%20Behavior%20Compass/18%20-%20Profiling%20Jack%20Kneel%20in%20Minutes.md)

### Frank Abagnale (Catch Me If You Can)
**Story:** He didn't know how to fly a plane, but convinced everyone he was a pilot.
**Lesson:** Leadership is certainty of self, not competence. [#34 The 5 Traits of Authority](transcripts/04%20-%20Confidence%2C%20Authority%20and%20Composure/34%20-%20The%205%20Traits%20of%20Authority.md)

### Truman's Experiment (The Truman Show)
**Story:** Truman sits in the car and predicts the order of every passerby — the lady on the bike, the man with flowers, the dented Volkswagen. They're on a loop. Later his wife reads a cocoa ad to the camera mid-argument.
**Lesson:** You wake up by testing reality, not by being told. [#71 The Truman Show and Waking Up](transcripts/09%20-%20Sartre%2C%20Performance%20and%20the%20Mask/71%20-%20The%20Truman%20Show%20and%20Waking%20Up.md)

### Sartre's Locked Room (No Exit)
**Story:** Three people are in a room. The door opens, but nobody leaves, because each needs to be seen a certain way by the others. A man begs a woman to say he's a good father. She does, but he knows she's lying to be liked.
**Lesson:** "The hell is needing to be seen by other people." [#68 Hell Is Other People - Sartre](transcripts/09%20-%20Sartre%2C%20Performance%20and%20the%20Mask/68%20-%20Hell%20Is%20Other%20People%20-%20Sartre.md)
